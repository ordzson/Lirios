Use case: ui-mockup
Asset type: north-star desktop landing page comp for a design-direction decision
Primary request: Create a polished, shippable first viewport for a Spanish online gift catalog in Quetzaltenango, Guatemala, provisionally named Lirios. The products are handmade flowers and arrangements made from pipe cleaners; use the three supplied images only as product/material references and preserve their visibly fuzzy bent-fiber construction. This direction is called “Álbum de pétalos”: the website behaves like an oversized botanical artist’s book opened on a table, not like a standard ecommerce hero.
Scene/backdrop: warm ivory cotton-paper spread occupying the entire 16:9 desktop viewport, with a fine central fold, translucent vellum overlaps and quiet edge shadows; no browser chrome.
Subject: one refined pipe-cleaner flower arrangement crosses the book fold at monumental scale, photographed from a slightly elevated editorial angle. It must unmistakably be chenille/pipe-cleaner craft, never real flowers, plastic flowers, LEGO, or felt.
Style/medium: high-fidelity web UI mockup blended with editorial product photography; elegant, tactile, slightly feminine but inclusive; curious, collectible, mature.
Composition/framing: slim top navigation integrated into the upper paper edge. Left page holds a small brand and a large two-line headline with one primary action. The arrangement bridges the center. Along the right page edge, three staggered physical page tabs make the catalog paths visible. Avoid a centered conventional split hero and avoid a grid of generic cards.
Lighting/mood: soft side light grazing the fibers so every fuzzy strand and bend is visible; calm morning atelier light.
Color palette: cotton ivory #F2EBDD, wine #6E263D, dusty rose #C98692, deep leaf #385442, near-black plum #211A1C. No gradients.
Materials/textures: cotton paper, vellum, blind embossing, velvet chenille fibers, restrained stitched binding.
Text (verbatim, no additional text): “Lirios”; navigation “Arreglos”, “Flores individuales”, “Personaliza”, “Seguir pedido”; headline “Flores que duran más que el momento.”; primary button “Ver arreglos”; right-edge tabs “ARREGLOS”, “FLORES”, “A TU MANERA”. Typography should combine a refined high-contrast editorial serif for the headline with a quiet humanist sans for navigation and labels. Keep all text readable and correctly spelled.
Interaction cues: the primary tab ARREGLOS is visibly selected; the other tabs promise individual flowers and custom arrangements. Controls should feel cut, folded, embossed, or tabbed from paper rather than floating pills.
Constraints: first commercial priority must be ready-made arrangements, then individual flowers, then personalized arrangements; no prices, promotions, testimonials, star ratings, shipping claims, fake logos, contact data, social proof, watermarks, or phone mockup. The product is the visual protagonist. Suitable as a measured web comp, not a mood board.
Avoid: childish craft-market styling, wedding cliché, luxury black-and-gold, generic pastel floral template, beige minimalism with tiny product, rounded-card dashboard, glassmorphism, gradients, excessive UI chrome, real fresh flowers.
